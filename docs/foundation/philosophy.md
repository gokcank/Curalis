# Philosophy

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental principles that guide every decision made throughout the Curalis project.

It serves as the project's compass, ensuring that product, engineering, and design decisions remain aligned over time.

When documentation appears to conflict, this philosophy should guide the final decision.

---

# Vision

Create a medication management application that people can trust with one of the most important aspects of their daily lives.

Trust is earned through reliability, transparency, privacy, and simplicity.

---

# Mission

Help people manage their medications safely, efficiently, and independently while respecting their privacy and maintaining complete control over their own data.

---

# Core Values

The following values define Curalis.

They are ordered by priority.

1. Medical Safety
2. User Privacy
3. User Ownership
4. Reliability
5. Simplicity
6. Accessibility
7. Transparency
8. Maintainability
9. Performance

Feature quantity is never a core value.

---

# Medical Safety First

Medical safety takes priority over every other concern.

No feature, optimization, or convenience should compromise user safety.

When uncertainty exists, communicate uncertainty instead of making assumptions.

---

# Privacy First

Medical information belongs to the user.

Collect the minimum amount of information necessary.

Never collect data simply because it may become useful later.

Privacy is a product feature.

---

# User Ownership

Users own their data.

Users control:

- their medications
- their reminders
- their history
- their notes
- their exports

The application exists to assist users—not to control their information.

---

# Local First

The application should remain fully functional without internet access whenever possible.

Cloud services should enhance the experience rather than become a requirement.

Offline capability is a design principle.

---

# Reliability Over Features

A smaller feature set that works consistently is preferable to a larger feature set that behaves unpredictably.

Reliability builds trust.

---

# Simplicity Over Complexity

Complexity should never be introduced without a measurable benefit.

Prefer solutions that are:

- understandable
- maintainable
- testable

Simple systems are easier to improve.

---

# Accessibility by Default

Accessibility should be part of every feature from the beginning.

Users should not need special versions of the application.

Inclusive design benefits everyone.

---

# Transparency

Users should understand:

- what the application does
- what it does not do
- where information comes from
- how data is used

Hidden behavior reduces trust.

---

# Human-Centered Design

Technology should adapt to people.

People should not have to adapt to technology.

Interfaces should reduce cognitive effort rather than increase it.

---

# Evidence Over Assumptions

Important decisions should be based on evidence.

Assumptions should be validated through:

- documentation
- testing
- research
- user feedback

Avoid designing around guesses.

---

# Long-Term Thinking

Short-term convenience should not compromise long-term maintainability.

Every architectural decision should consider future evolution.

Avoid solutions that require unnecessary rewrites.

---

# Progressive Enhancement

Build a solid foundation first.

Additional capabilities should extend the application without weakening existing functionality.

---

# AI as an Assistant

Artificial intelligence is a productivity tool.

AI should:

- automate repetitive work
- summarize information
- improve usability

AI must never replace medical professionals or make medical decisions on behalf of users.

---

# Decision-Making

When multiple valid solutions exist, prioritize them in this order:

1. Medical Safety
2. Privacy
3. User Ownership
4. Reliability
5. Simplicity
6. Accessibility
7. Maintainability
8. Performance
9. Feature Completeness

---

# Non-Goals

Curalis does not aim to become:

- A diagnostic platform
- A telemedicine platform
- A prescription service
- A hospital information system
- A replacement for healthcare professionals
- A social network
- An advertising platform
- A data collection platform

---

# Success

Success is measured by:

- User trust
- Reliable reminders
- Stable performance
- Safe behavior
- Respect for privacy
- Long-term maintainability

Success is not measured by the number of implemented features.
